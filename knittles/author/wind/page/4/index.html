Improvised ruffle scarf

12/15/2011knittingbinding off, scarvesWind Cowles

My son and I wanted to give something to his pre-school teacher as a thank you to all she’s done for him over the last year and a half. So, he picked out the yarn, and I knitted her a (surprise, surprise!) ruffle scarf.

As always, even with repeated projects, there is a lesson for me – this one is that when your yarn runs out while you are halfway into binding off (binding off!)Â a busy, ruffly project, you can actually bind off the stitches without the extra yarn. Meaning, out of desperation, I simply stopped using the working yarn, and just took the stitches straight from the right needle onto the left, then bound them off in the usual way. Redbeard could not tell where I had started this foul (er, I mean, inventive) deed, and so I think that it worked out okay, in a desperate-times-desperate-measures sort of way.

Button holes, smutton holes

12/07/2011knittingbuttonhole, knitting, scarvesWind Cowles

I just finished the manly-button scarf! I’m not sure that I did the cast-on part correctly (in fact, I’m certain that I did not do it the way that I was instructed to), butÂ  even still I am shocked at how easy it actually was in the end. After some poking around online, I went with this set of instructions, and all went smoothly, almost.

My mother once told me that she would gladly do whatever was necessary to not have to parallel park, even if that meant walking five miles in the snow up hill both ways. I never quite understood this until yesterday, when I realized that I feel the exact same way about casting on. I know exactly one way to cast on (long-tail). And, I’m really, really happy with this cast-on. I will go to great lengths (ha ha) to use this cast on, even when asked to cast on 100+ stitches.

Looking at how to do the “required” cable cast on for this particular button hole has not changed my mind. I spent a fair amount of time saying “oh, no no no” as I watched the instructions unfold. Then, I spent way too long trying to figure out how to use a long-tail cast on. In the end, defeated, I sort of flailed around with the needles in a way that is sort of like a cable cast on – I didn’t make a slip knot, but just took the next stitch, knitted into it, then pulled that stitch onto the left needle, knit into it, slipped it back on, etc. I don’t think it would work for very many stitches, but it worked for the 4 that I needed!

I don’t have a good picture of the button hole itself, but I’m pretty happy with it. Here’s a picture of the final scarf, folded up and ready to wrap!

Like I need another hobby

12/06/2011Fiberfelting, gnomes, needle felting, woolWind Cowles

But this one is fiber related, so that’s okay, right?

Last weekend I went to a local artisan’s festival here in town, and our local Waldorf-inspired school had a table filled with fantastic and fantastical things. I ended up buying a couple of lovely magical wands for the daughters of friends of mine (coincidentally made by another friend of mine, though I didn’t know that at the time). But, the thing that really caught my attention was the population of felted gnomes. Unspeakably cute and cuddly, I handled them way more than is decent. Yet, in the end, I could not quite bring myself to buy one. Something in my heart made me understand that I wanted – needed – to make my own, although I didn’t have the faintest clue how they were put together (fairy dust and love?).

If you aren’t familiar with this type of felted project, let me direct your attention to Felting Dreams (an etsy store), which has wonderful examples.

How I love the internet. Within 48 hours, I now know that what I want to learn how to do is called needle felting, and I have identified two books and about five websites that look promising. Oh, and I ordered a set of felting needles and a felting pad. Because what do I need? Another hobby. (And my very own felt gnome!)
