We’ve decamped to upstate New York for the rest of the summer, and determined to visit a few of the farm breweries that are popping up all over the area.Â  So we headed over to Hopshire Farm & Brewery, planning to taste their beers and have a picnic dinner.Â  We were excited to see hop bines growing in the front of the brewery, but disappointed to see the picnic tables mentioned on their website were just off the parking lot, and in full sun.

Coming in the front door, we entered a cozy square taproom.Â  On the left was the brewery, and on the right was a small side room with a table and some toys for kids.Â  Luckily, we were there early and were the only ones there, so we were able to eat in the side room.

At the back was a bar with seven beers on tap, divided into mellow, middling, and mighty:

Mellow

  • Beehave: a light and refreshing ale, made with local honey.Â  It has a nice sweetness (without the heavier body of maltier beers), and reminds us of a honey cream ale from back home we quite like.
  • Brambles: a straw-colored wheat beer, with just a hint of earthy raspberry.Â  Has more wheat twang than I care for, but Wind quite liked it.

Middling

  • Daddy ‘O: an English pale ale, but with more fruity yeast character than typical, and a nice Fuggles finish.
  • Shire Ale: not as dark as the typical near-black Scottish ale you often see, but with more malt complexity.Â  In particular, this beer has more dark malt roastiness than typical.Â  This was another beer that Wind liked more than I did.Â 
  • Hamlet of Varna: a 5% session IPA.Â  This seems like a popular style this summer and we’ve just had a few of these (like Southern Tier’s Farmer’s Tan).Â  I do appreciate the trend away from big IPAs, though they do suffer in rapid-fire tastings because, at the end of the day, they are weaker beers.Â  So I liked Hamlet of Varna quite a bit, but NearVarna even more.

Mighty

  • NearVarna: a wonderful American IPA in the malty East-coast style.Â  Especially immediately after the milder Hamlet of Varna, this is a fantastic hop showcase.
  • Sympathy: a rich, full-bodied Belgian golden ale with characteristic estery profile.Â Â  My favorite of the bunch, and the strongest.

After we’d eaten and enjoyed a couple of half-pints, we got a couple of growlers filled.Â  By this time, the taproom had filled up, but the wait wasn’t long.Â  Alas, even though they seemed to be sealed properly, both growlers were undercarbonated.Â  We had the first the same night, and the second the next night.Â  The beer still tasted fine, but obviously not as good as in the taproom.

  • The Cream Ale was a good example of one of my favorite styles — just a light pale ale, with medium body.Â  Cream ale is one of my favorites to drink on a hot summer day, and we got a growler to take home with us.
  • I didn’t care for the Southern English Mild — I think I’ve only had dark mild, and this was very different.
  • The Weizenbock was very good, spicy and malty.Â  There was none of the wheaty tang that often spoils wheat beers for me.

  • The Harvest ale was rich and satisfying.
  • The beer I liked best was their Wee Heavy.Â  It’s one of my favorite styles, and theirs wasn’t too peaty and had a nice caramel note to it that I really liked.Â Â  Of course, this was their special small-batch beer, in a small glass and not available for growlers.
