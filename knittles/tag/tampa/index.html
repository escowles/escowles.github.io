Cigar City Brewpub

08/20/2013Beer, Foodbeer, brewpub, florida, tampaescowles

We finally made it down to the Cigar City Brewpub recently.Â  It opened in April, and we’ve been down to Tampa a few times since then, but it didn’t work out until last weekend.

I was a little worried, since I read some pretty mixed reviews online, including complaints about lackluster food and spotty service.Â  As it turned out, we all enjoyed it and had no complaints.Â  The food menu had a lot of variety and some nice Cuban influences (we had black bean cakes and a picadillo meat loaf sandwich with yuca fries).Â  Everything was very tasty.

The beer menu had a handful of standards from the main brewery, but was mostly the beers brewed at the brewpub, including some special variants and one cask option (that sold out just before we got there, natch).Â  Wind had a light, crisp beer/cider mix (kind of like a shandy/radler), and I had a rich, spicy golden ale and (my favorite) some Beer Myth Bock, which was a lot like a sweet stout.

The atmosphere was nice and relaxed.Â  The decor was very typical pub — mostly dark wood and fabric, but there was enough natural light coming in to keep it from being cavelike.Â  They did have a couple of TVs playing sports, but luckily without sound.

We were also happy to see they had a cooler of bottles by the front door, well stocked with Cucumber Saison and Good Gourd.Â  I’d called all over town earlier in the week looking for Marshall Zhukov Stout and Good Gourd — and the few places that got any sold out in less than an hour, even with 1-bottle-per-customer limits.

Hunaphu Day 2013

03/10/2013BeerCigar City Brewing, florida, Hunahpu, tampaescowles

We headed down to Tampa for Cigar City’s release of Hunahpu.Â  This is no ordinary bottle release — this is their most sought-after beer, the line starts forming up the night before, and if you’re lucky enough to get any at all, you only get three bottles.Â  In short, it’s more of a festival than a bottle release.

So we got a hotel a few blocks away, and I planned to get up at 5am and walk over.Â  I couldn’t sleep (strange bed or anticipation?), so I gave up shortly before 4am and headed over.Â  There was a small line already, with most of the people camped out, and I estimated I was about 75th in line.Â  There was a steady trickle of people joining the line, which quickly stretched down the block and into the parking lot of the shopping center around the corner.Â  So they wound up handing out the golden wristbands early (prompting lots of panicked phone calls to people who were supposed to join people in line), and they opened the gates just after 7am.

Cigar City blocked off their parking lot, and setup several trailers to serve beer, in addition to the two bars normally operating in their tasting room.Â  There were about a dozen food trucks, a few merch booths, live music, and some tents and carnival-style amusements way in the back by their new brewhouse.Â  The taplist was very impressive.Â  Everybody was in a good and accommodating mood.

The only problem was the size: there were just too many people in too small a space.Â  Lines for everything were long, snaking around and into the end of other lines.Â  Navigating the space was difficult because the lines were taking up so much space.Â  The police showed up shortly after 6am — I thought they were going to complain about people walking in the street, or drinking on the sidewalk, but they were apparently upset about the line winding around the block, which prompted the early opening.

They’ve already said the event is too big to hold at the brewery, and might be held at a larger venue or spread over several days.Â  But other than having a larger space, I think there are a couple of other things they might do to help make things go more smoothly:

  • Post the master taplist — I was having my first glass of Hunahpu in the tasting room, when someone at my table noticed a folded-up piece of paper which turned out to be the master taplist.Â  This was an invaluable source of information for us to figure out which of the serving points had which beers.Â  Without this, you would have to walk up to each one and read the paper taplist at the bar to figure out where to go.Â  Maybe this doesn’t get sorted out soon enough to be printed up and handed out to everyone.Â  But posting it online, or even setting up a bulletin board somewhere so people could consult it would be nice.
  • They required tokens instead of cash for all the outside bars, which definitely sped up service.Â  But they were letting people get samples and several drinks per person at a time, both of which slow things down.Â  Of course, more space and more service points is the real solution to the problem.
  • I’m also not a big fan of first-come-first-served-line-around-the-block as a strategy for rare bottle releases.Â  I think it would make a lot more sense to have a lottery for tickets.Â  Or auction off a portion of the bottles and/or tickets.Â  Maybe a combination of all three — they could have a first-come-first-served ticket sale for a majority of the tickets, and then an auction and/or lottery for the rest.Â  But whatever the method, selling tickets ahead of time would make it much easier to plan for the number of people who were coming.

Sour Beer Fest

06/17/2012Beerbeer, berliner weisse, saison, tampa, tastingescowles

I went to the Sour, Lambic & Berlinner Weisse festival at Cajun Cafe on the Bayou in Tampa this weekend. Â The flyer listed an impressive selection of sour beer, about half from Florida, about half from Belgium, and a few others (including BFM from Switzerland and Ommegang from New York). Â There were also a few other domestic bottles that were not on the program (more on that later).

There were a couple of tables run by shops, with a wide selection of imports. Â And there were several local breweries, brewpubs, and homebrew groups with broader selection of their brews. Â I think my favorites of the afternoon were:

  • BFM’s Abbaye de Saint Bon-Chien (2007): they actually had four years of this brew (2006, 2007, 2009, 2010), with pretty significant differences between the years. Â 2006 was very funky but not very sour at all. Â The 2009 and 2010 bottles were good, with varying sourness, funkiness and other flavors. Â But the 2007 was really strong and tart, with just enough funkiness to make it interesting.
  • Hanssens’ Oude Gueuze: There were a several bottles from Hanssens, with various fruits. Â But I think the plain gueuze was the best. Â A very clean and sour gueuze.
  • 3 Fonteinen’s Schaerbeekse Kriek: This was the one kriek I really wanted to have when we were in Brussels, but couldn’t find. Â I was not disappointed to have it at last: it had a great, almost gritty cherry base, with a big tart lambic bite. Â Probably the best kriek I’ve had, with a nice combination of cherry and lambic flavors (instead of just having one of them prevail as often happens).
  • Cascade’s Sang Noir: I was really glad to see a few Cascade bottles were there. Â This was a very nice, strongly cherry-flavored kriek, with a richness and creaminess that you don’t often see in sour beers.
  • Mikkellers’s Funky E *: The first glass of this I had was terrible and watery — I think it probably fell over in the ice bath. Â But they opened up a second bottle and I’m glad I gave it another chance. Â It had a nice oakiness, and more malty sweetness than most sours.

Another real treat came just after the second glass of Funky E * — I saw the server pull a bottle ofÂ Pliny the ElderÂ out and put it in the ice bath under the counter. Â I don’t know if he was planning on sharing, but I said “Hey! I saw that!” and he gave me a glass. Â Within a few seconds, a crowd formed and the bottle was soon gone. Â It definitely lived up to the reputation as being a very balanced, world-class IPA. Â And I was a little amused to see the biggest commotion at the sour fest was for an IPA.

In fact, there were really only two disappointments of the afternoon:

  • I’d never had Berliner Weisse before, and I had several examples from a range of breweries. Â I didn’t find any of them better than drinkable, and most of them I poured out. Â So I can scratch that style off my list.
  • I didn’t like the offerings from Cigar City (one of the berliner weisses) or Saint Somewhere (a strange refermentation of their Saison Athene). Â I generally count on them to make beers I like (and sometimes love), so that was a little sad.
