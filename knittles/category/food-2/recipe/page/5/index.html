Pork and Beans

01/02/2018Recipemains, pork, soupescowles

1 1/4 cups multi-bean soup mix
6 cups water
2 Tbsp. olive oil
1 onion, chopped
2 cloves garlic, minced
1 lb. pork roast, cubed
1 bay leaf
1 Tbsp. brown sugar
1 tsp. thyme
1/2 tsp. black pepper
1 tsp. salt

  1. Rinse beans and soak overnight in 3 cups water; or boil beans in 6 cups water for 5 minutes, then cover and soak for 1 hour.
  2. Rinse beans and return to pot with 6 cups water. Loosely cover and simmer for 2 hours.
  3. Saute onion and garlic in oil, then add to beans.
  4. Sear pork and add to beans.
  5. Add remaining ingredients and simmer until meat and beans are tender, approx. 1 hour.  Add water as necessary to adjust thickness.

Snickerdoodles

01/02/2018Recipecookies, dessertescowles

1 ½ cups sugar
1 cup butter, softened
2 eggs
2 ¾ cups flour
1 Tbsp. baking powder
½ tsp. salt
¼ cup sugar
2 tsp. cinnamon

  1. Mix sugar, butter, and eggs in large bowl.
  2. Add flour, baking powder, and salt.
  3. Form dough into 1¼” balls and roll in cinnamon sugar.
  4. Bake at 400F (205C) 8-10 minutes, until set (they will not look quite done).

Praline Truffles

01/02/2018Recipechocolate, dessert, nutsescowles

Praline:

1 cup hazelnuts (or mixture of nuts)
½ cup sugar
2 Tbsp. water

  • Bake hazelnuts in a single layer 10-15 minutes at 350°F.
  • Wrap in kitchen towel and rest for 1 minute.
  • Rub hazelnuts in towel to remove loose skins (don’t worry about skins that don’t come off) and cool completely.
  • Combine sugar and water in saucepan and cook over medium heat until it is clear amber and the edges start to foam, about 5 minutes.
  • Remove from heat, add hazelnuts, and pour onto parchment-lined baking sheet and allow to cool completely, about 20 minutes.
  • Break up and process in process in food processor until paste.

Filling:

4 oz. hazelnut praline
3 oz. milk chocolate
1 oz. semisweet chocolate

  • Microwave chocolate 30 sec. at a time, stirring between each heating until melted.
  • Combine with hazelnut praline, mix thoroughly, and chill.

Truffles:

2 oz. milk chocolate
2 oz. semisweet chocolate

  • Microwave 2 oz. of milk chocolate and 1 oz. of semisweet chocolate 30 sec. at a time, stirring between each heating, until smooth and shiny.
  • Add remaining chocolate and stir until smooth.
  • Cut filling into even sized pieces, coat with tempered chocolate, and place on parchment-lined baking sheet and chill.
  • Allow to harden for a few minutes and serve at room temperature.

Shepherd’s Pie

01/02/2018Recipebeef, British, mainsescowles

Filling:

1 Tbsp. butter
1/2 onion, finely chopped
1 carrot, finely chopped
1 lb. ground beef
2 Tbsp. flour
1 Tbsp. ketchup
2 Tbsp. heavy cream
3/8 cup chicken broth
3/8 cup beer
1 Tbsp. soy sauce
1 tsp. thyme, minced
1/2 cup frozen peas

  • Saute onion and carrot in butter until onions are caramelized and tender, about 8 min.
  • Add beef, season with salt and pepper, and cook until browned, about 6 min.
  • Add flour and tomato paste, and cook until paste starts to darken, about 1 min.
  • Add cream and cook about 1 min.
  • Add broth, beer, soy sauce and thyme. Simmer until thickened but still saucy, about 5 min.
  • Remove from heat, add peas, season with salt and pepper, and add to casserole dish.

Topping:

1 lb. potatoes
1 Tbsp. butter
3 Tbsp. heavy cream

  • Cut potatoes and boil until tender. Drain and mash with butter and cream.
  • Spread potatoes over filling, smooth top and then make ridges with a fork.
  • Bake at 375F (190C) for 15 min., until bubbling.
  • Broil (high) for 3-5 min., until golden brown.
  • Remove from oven and cool for 10 min. before serving.

Sausage Gravy

01/02/2018Recipecondiments, gravy, pork, sausageescowles

sausage:

1 lb. ground pork
1 tsp. sage
1 tsp. rosemary
1/2 tsp. thyme
1 tsp. salt
1/2 tsp. pepper
1/8 tsp. cayenne
1/8 tsp. nutmeg
1/8 tsp. cloves
2 tsp. brown sugar

  • Mix all ingredients

gravy:

1/4 cup flour
2 cups milk
1/2 tsp. salt
1/2 tsp. pepper

  • Brown sausage until finely crumbled.
  • Add flour and mix well.  The sausage drippings should be fully absorbed by the flour.
  • Add milk and spices, and heat on medium heat until bubbling and thickened.
  • Serve over biscuits.

I generally make 1/2 lb. of the sausage into patties and brown the other half in the same pan, then halve the rest of the recipe.

Saison Bruxellensis

01/02/2018Beer, Recipebrett, saisonescowles

8½ lbs. Belgian Pilsner malt
½ lb. Vienna malt
½ lb. Munich malt (60L)
1 lb. clear candi sugar (syrup or rocks)

1 oz. Styrian Goldings hop pellets

½ oz. East Kent Goldings hop pellets
1 tsp. Irish moss
½ tsp. bitter orange peel

¼ oz. East Kent Goldings hop pellets

WLP 565 Belgian saison yeast
WLP 650 Brett Bruxellensis yeast

  • Heat 10½ quarts of water to 180°F, add to mash tun and rest until temperature drops to 170°F.
  • Mash grains at 150°-156°F for 60 minutes, until starch test shows conversion is complete.
  • Collect wort slowly while sparing with 5 gallons of 200°F water.
  • Collect 6.5 gallons, with a gravity of 1.040 (10.0 Brix).
  • Add candi sugar to bring gravity up to 1.048 (11.8 Brix).
  • Bring to a boil.
  • Boil 30 minutes and add SG hop pellets.
  • Boil 45 minutes and add ½ oz. EKG hop pellets, Irish moss and orange peel.
  • Boil 10 minutes and add ¼ oz. EKG hop pellets.
  • Boil 5 minutes and remove from heat.
  • Chill using wort chiller, rack to sterile fermentor, and dilute to 1.057 (14 Brix).  Or rack to sterile fermentor while hot, dilute, and chill with ice bath or allow to cool to room temperature overnight.
  • Pitch yeast when temperature is below 80°F and seal with airlock.  Fermentation should begin within 24 hours.
  • Ferment until gravity drops to 1.017 (8 Brix).
  • Keg and force carbonate, or bottle (I use Northern Brewer’s priming sugar calculator to determine how much priming sugar to use).

Yield: 5 gallons, ABV: 5.5%, IBU: 27.5

Depression-Era Vegan Chocolate Cake

01/02/2018Recipecake, chocolate, dessertescowles

1½ cups flour
¹/3 cup cocoa
1 tsp. baking soda
1 cup sugar
½ tsp. salt
5 Tbsp. vegetable oil
1 cup water
1½ tsp. vanilla
1 Tbsp. cider or white vinegar

  • Mix dry ingredients.
  • In a separate bowl, whisk wet ingredients.
  • Combine and whisk until smooth.
  • Tap mixing bowl or drop from 6 inches to shake out air bubbles.
  • Bake at 350°F for 25-30 minutes, until toothpick comes out clean.
  • Cool before removing from pan.
  • Dust with powdered sugar or frost as desired.

Chicken Soup

01/02/2018Recipechicken, mains, soupescowles

11/2 Tbsp. butter
1 lb. boneless skinless chicken breast
1 onion
2 carrots
3 stalks celery
2 cloves garlic

2 bay leaves
1/2 tsp. thyme (or five sprigs fresh)
1/4 tsp. rosemary (or two sprigs fresh)
1/4 tsp. turmeric
salt and pepper
3 cups chicken stock or bouillon

1/3 cup milk or heavy cream

  • Cube chicken and chop vegetables.
  • In 6-qt. pot, melt butter.  Sauté chicken and vegetables until chicken is browned, about 5 minutes.
  • Reduce heat, add herbs and stock, and simmer until chicken is tender, about 20 minutes.
  • Add cream and simmer for 15 minutes, keeping simmer very low to avoid curdling the cream.

Chocolate Ginger Crackle Cookies

06/08/2012Food, Recipechocolate, cookies, dessert, ginger, wineWind Cowles

So, somewhat off-topic, but in the public interest (plus I’ve got nothing on the knitting or beer front to report)…

I started making these cookies in 1997, when I was a graduate student. They have been a solid hit with almost everyone: People who like cookies, people who don’t like cookies, people who think they are too old for cookies, children, people who think they don’t like ginger, ginger-fantatics… Plus, these cookies are consistently good enough that the obligatory “taste” cookie is really just pro-forma.

Anyway, so as I baked a batch tonight for game night (yay, game night!), I realized that I should share. The recipe is at the bottom.

First, let me say that these cookies are sweetish and a little spicy, and they go pretty well with wine (which explains why I found the recipe in Wine and Food originally).  I can’t remember what the recipe used to look like – I suspect that I have probably simplified it a lot over the years, and I know that I will adjust the amount of ginger, depending on my audience. Also, be warned – our local grocery store today informed us that candied ginger is a “seasonal” item. Seriously? In this whole “globalized” market place, we still think that anything can’t be sold any time, anywhere?

Anyway… these are refrigerator cookies and the prep always reminds me a little of peanut butter cookies and snickerdoodles. Be prepared to wait at least 2 hours between when you mix up the batter (dough? eh, never can keep those straight) and when it is ready – and then be prepared to get your hands all chocolatified as you form the dough/batter into balls.

Ingredients (makes ~24 cookies)

1c sugar
6 Tbsp butter, melted
2 eggs
1/4 cup + 2 Tbsp unsweetened cocoa powder
1 tsp vanilla extract
1 cup flour
1 tsp baking powder
pinch salt
1/2 cup semi-sweet chocolate chips
1/3 cup candied ginger, minced
1/3 cup confectioner’s sugar

Instructions

1. Beat sugar with butter, then add eggs, cocoa and vanilla until smooth and creamy

2. Add flour, baking powder and salt (you can just dump it all in together) and mix until blended (about 2 minutes with an electric mixer)

3. Stir in chocolate chips and ginger

4. Cover and refrigerate overnight (yeah, right) or at least two hours

5. Preheat the oven to 350F

6. Lightly grease your cookie sheets if they are prone to things sticking to them

7. Form dough into 1″ balls and then coat generously with the confectioner’s sugar

8. Place on the the cookie sheet with a little room to expand (about 1″ apart should be good)

9. Bake 10-12 minutes, tops should be crinkly.

Note #1: Don’t over bake! These things are just like peanut butter cookies: If they look done in the oven then they are over-done. I’ve even taken these out when they looked a tiny bit wet in the cracks, and they were fine.

Note #2: I let the cookies rest for about 30 seconds or so on the cookie sheet before I move them off to a cooling rack.

Note #3: I hope you enjoy them as much as we do!
